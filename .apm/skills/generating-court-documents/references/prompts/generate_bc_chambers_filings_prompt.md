### Document 2: Form 32 (Rule 8-1(4)) - Notice of Application
```text
FORM 32 (RULE 8-1 (4))

[Style of Proceeding - Form 22]

NOTICE OF APPLICATION

Name(s) of applicant(s): The Plaintiff, Acme Enterprises Ltd.

To: The Defendants, Pacific Developments Corp. and John Doe
    c/o their counsel of record, Vance Barristers & Solicitors, Suite 2200 - 555 West Hastings Street, Vancouver, BC

TAKE NOTICE that an application will be made by the Applicant to the presiding associate judge in chambers 
at the Courthouse, 800 Smithe Street, Vancouver, British Columbia, on November 15, 2026, at 9:45 AM, for the 
orders set out in Part 1 below.

PART 1: ORDERS SOUGHT

1. The Defendant, Pacific Developments Corp., shall, within fourteen (14) days of the date of this Order, 
   amend its List of Documents delivered on August 12, 2026, and deliver a Supplementary List of Documents 
   in Form 22, verified by affidavit, listing all documents in its possession, custody, or control relating 
   to any matter in question in this action, and specifically:
   (a) All unredacted quarterly project accounting ledgers, progress draw requests, and certified cost 
       audits relating to the development project located at 1200 Burrard Street, Vancouver, BC, for 
       the period covering January 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026;
   (b) All internal correspondence, emails, and memoranda between the Defendant John Doe and project 
       quantity surveyor Apex Cost Consultants Inc. regarding budget overruns and contingency drawdowns;
   (c) All banking statements and wire transfer records for Canadian Commercial Bank Account No. 4410-*** 
       evidencing the disbursement of the $1,500,000 contingency reserve fund.

2. The Defendant, Pacific Developments Corp., shall make the documents listed in paragraph 1 above available 
   for inspection and copying by counsel for the Plaintiff within seven (7) days of the delivery of the said 
   Supplementary List of Documents, pursuant to Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(15).

3. In the event that any of the documents described in paragraph 1 are claimed to be privileged, the Defendant 
   shall deliver a supplementary Part 3 listing setting out the specific legal grounds of privilege claimed, 
   the author, recipient, date, and general subject matter of each document with sufficient particularity to 
   enable the Plaintiff and the Court to evaluate the claim.

4. Costs of this application shall be in the cause.

PART 2: FACTUAL BASIS

1. The Plaintiff and the Corporate Defendant entered into a written Joint Venture Development Agreement 
   dated January 15, 2025 (the "Agreement"), governing the construction and financing of a mixed-use residential 
   tower at 1200 Burrard Street, Vancouver, BC.

2. Pursuant to Clause 4.2 of the Agreement, the Corporate Defendant was appointed Managing Partner and was 
   under a strict contractual and fiduciary obligation to maintain transparent, unredacted accounting books, 
   records, and project bank statements, and to provide quarterly reconciliations to the Plaintiff.

3. On May 10, 2026, the Plaintiff contributed $1,500,000 into the project contingency account pursuant to a 
   cash-call notice issued by the Individual Defendant, John Doe.

4. On August 12, 2026, the Corporate Defendant delivered its List of Documents. In Part 1 of the list, the 
   Defendant listed project summaries but entirely omitted the underlying bank statements, detailed progress 
   billing sheets, and general accounting ledgers.

5. On September 14, 2026, counsel for the Plaintiff wrote to the Defendants demanding production of the 
   accounting records and unredacted progress draws. By letter dated September 28, 2026, the Defendants 
   refused production, asserting that the underlying documents were "commercially sensitive" and that the 
   summaries provided were sufficient.

6. The documents sought are directly material to the core allegations in paragraphs 14 through 22 of the 
   Notice of Civil Claim, wherein the Plaintiff pleads breach of contract, unauthorized redirection of 
   contingency funds, and breaches of fiduciary duties.

7. The requested production is proportionate under Rule 1-3, having regard to the amount involved ($1,500,000), 
   the commercial importance of the issues, and the fact that the records are kept electronically by the 
   Defendant and can be produced in PDF format with minimal expense.

PART 3: LEGAL BASIS

1. Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(11): The Court's jurisdiction to order delivery of a supplementary list of 
   documents where an existing list is incomplete or inadequate.
2. Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(18): The Court's power to order production and inspection of specified documents 
   or classes of documents in a party's possession or control.
3. Supreme Court Civil Rule 1-3: The object of the Rules to secure the just, speedy, and inexpensive determination 
   of every proceeding on its merits, in a manner proportionate to the amount involved and the issues.
4. Practice Direction 72 (PD-72) and Practice Direction 73 (Virtual Chambers).
5. Leading Jurisprudence: Kalin v. Campbell, 2014 BCCA 441 at paras. 22-26; Biehl v. Strang, 2010 BCSC 1391; 
   and Smith v. Jones, 2024 BCCA 123.

PART 4: MATERIAL TO BE RELIED ON

1. Affidavit #1 of Jane Smith, sworn October 25, 2026, and the exhibits attached thereto.
2. The pleadings and proceedings filed herein, including the Notice of Civil Claim filed June 15, 2026, 
   and the Response to Civil Claim filed July 20, 2026.
3. The Defendant Pacific Developments Corp.'s List of Documents dated August 12, 2026.

The applicant estimates that the application will take twenty-five (25) minutes.

Dated: October 29, 2026
                                                        _____________________________________________
                                                        Jane Smith, Counsel for the Plaintiff (Applicant)
```

### Document 3: Form 33 (Rule 8-1(9)) - Application Response
```text
FORM 33 (RULE 8-1 (9))

[Style of Proceeding - Form 22]

APPLICATION RESPONSE

Application response of: The Defendants, Pacific Developments Corp. and John Doe (the "Respondents")

PART 1: ORDERS CONSENTED TO
The Respondents consent to the granting of the orders requested in paragraphs: None.

PART 2: ORDERS OPPOSED
The Respondents oppose the granting of the orders requested in paragraphs 1, 2, 3, and 4 of Part 1 
of the Notice of Application.

PART 3: ORDERS ON TERMS
The Respondents propose that the application be dismissed, or in the alternative, that production of any 
internal banking records be subject to a strict confidentiality undertaking in the form of the Canadian 
Commercial Protective Order model, with commercially sensitive sub-contractor pricing formulas redacted.

PART 4: FACTUAL BASIS

1. The Corporate Defendant has already delivered a comprehensive List of Documents comprising 487 documents 
   disclosing all executed construction contracts, architect sign-off certificates, and municipal permits.

2. The Plaintiff has been provided with certified monthly summary accounting reports prepared by independent 
   auditors, which demonstrate that all cash-call funds were allocated to active trade contractor accounts.

3. The granular daily banking records sought by the Plaintiff contain proprietary banking arrangements, line of 
   credit pricing terms, and confidential subcontractor fee margins that are completely irrelevant to the 
   pleaded issues in this action.

4. Compelling the unredacted disclosure of these proprietary records would give the Plaintiff, an active 
   commercial competitor in the downtown Vancouver development market, an unfair commercial advantage.

PART 5: LEGAL BASIS

1. Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(1): A party is only required to list documents that could be used by any party 
   at trial to prove or disprove a material fact, or that assist in determining that fact.
2. Supreme Court Civil Rule 1-3 (Proportionality): Ordering supplementary production of hundreds of internal 
   banking slips and raw communications would be disproportionate, oppressive, and unnecessary.
3. Dorfman v. Rosen, 2011 BCSC 1489 at para. 17: Production will not be ordered where the application represents 
   a speculative fishing expedition into proprietary business operations.

PART 6: MATERIAL TO BE RELIED ON

1. Affidavit #1 of John Doe, sworn November 3, 2026.
2. The Response to Civil Claim filed July 20, 2026.

The Respondents estimate that the application will take twenty (20) minutes.

Dated: November 4, 2026
                                                        _____________________________________________
                                                        David Vance, Counsel for the Defendants
```

### Document 7: Full-Text Legal Authority (Leading BCCA Decision)
```text
CITATION: Kalin v. Campbell, 2014 BCCA 441
DATE: 20141118
DOCKET: CA041782

COURT OF APPEAL FOR BRITISH COLUMBIA

BETWEEN:
    David Kalin
                                                                    Appellant (Plaintiff)
AND:
    John Campbell and Coastline Ventures Ltd.
                                                                    Respondents (Defendants)

Before: The Honourable Chief Justice Bauman
        The Honourable Madam Justice Saunders
        The Honourable Mr. Justice Groberman

On appeal from: An order of the Supreme Court of British Columbia, entered March 14, 2014 
(Kalin v. Campbell, 2014 BCSC 412).

REASONS FOR JUDGMENT OF THE COURT:

[1] This appeal concerns the scope of pre-trial document disclosure under Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1, 
and the test to be applied when a party seeks supplementary production of underlying financial records 
in a commercial joint venture dispute.

[2] Rule 7-1(1) requires a party to deliver a list of all documents that are or have been in the party's 
possession or control and that could be used by any party at trial to prove or disprove a material fact. 
Where an opposing party establishes that the list is incomplete or that relevant documents exist that have 
been withheld, Rule 7-1(11) and Rule 7-1(18) grant the court broad discretionary authority to order a 
supplementary list and production for inspection.

[3] The associate judge below held that the plaintiff was obliged to accept summary spreadsheet reports 
prepared by the defendant's internal bookkeeper, reasoning that unredacted raw bank records were commercially 
sensitive. We respectfully disagree.

[4] Where a fiduciary or contractual duty of accounting is pleaded in relation to designated joint venture funds, 
the underlying bank statements and source transaction vouchers are fundamentally material. A party claiming an 
accounting is not required to accept their opponent's self-selected summaries. Transparency is the bedrock of 
the disclosure process.

[5] Nor does a generalized claim of "commercial sensitivity" preclude disclosure. Where sensitive proprietary 
terms exist, the appropriate mechanism is a confidentiality undertaking or protective order, not an outright 
refusal to list or produce.

[6] Having regard to the proportionality principles in Rule 1-3, the production of electronic bank records 
presents negligible cost or burden. The appeal is allowed. The respondents shall deliver a verified supplementary 
list and produce the bank records within 14 days.

"The Honourable Chief Justice Bauman"
"The Honourable Madam Justice Saunders"
"The Honourable Mr. Justice Groberman"
```
