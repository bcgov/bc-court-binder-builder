REASONS FOR JUDGMENT OF THE COURT:

[1] This appeal addresses the interpretation and application of section 18 of the Federal Child Support 
Guidelines where a paying parent is the sole or controlling shareholder of a private corporation.

[2] Section 18(1) provides that where a spouse is a shareholder, director, or officer of a corporation 
and the court is of the opinion that the spouse's annual income as determined under section 16 does not 
fairly reflect all the money available to the spouse for the payment of child support, the court may 
determine the spouse's annual income by including all or part of the pre-tax income of the corporation.

[3] The appellant contends that corporate retained earnings should not be included where an accountant 
testifies that cash is held for business contingencies. We reject that broad proposition. The primary 
objective of the Guidelines is to ensure that children benefit from the financial means of both parents. 
A paying parent cannot insulate income from child support obligations by choosing to retain earnings within 
a controlled corporation unless a legitimate, pressing corporate business necessity is clearly proven.

[4] As Madam Justice Prowse observed, the onus rests squarely upon the shareholder spouse to demonstrate 
that corporate pre-tax income is required for ongoing business maintenance or capital expenditure rather 
than personal tax deferral or accumulation. Where that onus is not met, the court will attribute corporate 
retained income to the parent.

[5] Having reviewed the balance sheets of the appellant's enterprise, the chambers judge committed no error 
in attributing corporate income to the appellant for the purpose of fixing fair guideline support.

[6] The appeal is accordingly dismissed with costs to the respondent.
