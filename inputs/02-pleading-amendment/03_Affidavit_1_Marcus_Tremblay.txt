AFFIDAVIT #1 OF MARCUS TREMBLAY

I, Marcus Tremblay, Project Manager, of Suite 1800 - 1075 West Georgia Street, in the City of Vancouver,
in the Province of British Columbia, SWEAR (OR AFFIRM) THAT:

1. I am the Project Manager of the Plaintiff, Apex Structures Corp. ("Apex"), and as such I have personal
   knowledge of the facts and matters deposed to in this affidavit, except where stated to be based on
   information and belief, in which case I believe those facts and matters to be true.

2. Apex was the owner and developer of the four-storey commercial building at 4418 Kingsway, Burnaby,
   British Columbia (the "Project"). Apex retained the Defendant, Cascade General Contracting Ltd.
   ("Cascade"), as general contractor under a written construction contract dated June 3, 2024 (the
   "Prime Contract").

3. Cascade subcontracted the roofing work to the Proposed Added Defendant, Pro-Tech Roofing Systems Inc.
   ("Pro-Tech") under a subcontract dated August 19, 2024. Now produced and shown to me and marked as
   Exhibit "A" to this my affidavit is the proposed Amended Notice of Civil Claim, which Apex seeks leave
   to file and which adds Pro-Tech as a defendant (the "Proposed Amended Claim").

4. Substantial performance of the Project was certified on March 28, 2025. Following that date, Apex's
   tenants and building operators reported water entering the roof assembly along the north and east
   elevations.

5. On July 9, 2025, I sent a written notice to Cascade describing the water ingress and requesting
   remediation under the warranty in the Prime Contract. Cascade responded by directing Apex to contact
   Pro-Tech as the installer of the roof assembly. I did so, and Pro-Tech attended once in August 2025 to
   apply a temporary sealant at the north parapet. The water ingress did not stop.

6. In September 2025, Apex retained Coastal Building Science Ltd. to investigate the roof assembly. The
   firm's inspection was delayed until September 2026 because of the scheduling of the tenant fit-out work
   and the need to obtain access to the occupied suites. A copy of the inspection report dated
   September 22, 2026, is now produced and shown to me and marked as Exhibit "B" to this my affidavit
   (the "Engineering Report").

7. The Engineering Report identifies two probable causes of the water ingress: (a) inadequate membrane
   lap detailing at the north and east parapet terminations, and (b) undersized roof drains that do not
   meet the design flow for the roof area. In the Engineering Report's opinion, both conditions are
   consistent with installation and design deficiencies in the roof assembly, and the report does not
   attribute either condition to tenant alterations.

8. Apex did not know, before receiving the Engineering Report, which party was responsible for the design
   of the roof drainage layout, or whether the drain sizing was a design or installation issue. Apex
   believes that the identification of those responsibilities could not reasonably have been made until
   the Engineering Report was completed.

9. Apex has incurred repair and remediation costs of approximately $412,000 to date in respect of the
   water damage, insulation replacement, and tenant fit-out restoration. Those costs continue to accrue,
   and the Engineering Report recommends that the drainage system be replaced in full.

10. I am advised by counsel, and I verily believe, that the proposed amendments do not introduce a new
    cause of action against Cascade, and that the amendments against Pro-Tech arise from the same roof
    assembly and the same series of events as the existing claim.

11. No examinations for discovery have been scheduled in this action, and no trial date has been set. The
    proposed amendments can be served and answered within the time limits in the Supreme Court Civil
    Rules without any adjournment of a trial date.

ROOF ASSEMBLY INSPECTION REPORT
Project: 4418 Kingsway, Burnaby, BC
Client: Apex Structures Corp.
Report date: September 22, 2026
Inspecting engineer: [Engineer name and P.Eng. number to be inserted]

1. SCOPE

   Visual and infrared inspection of the roof membrane, parapet terminations, and roof drainage system
   on the north and east elevations, performed on September 9 and 10, 2026. Destructive testing was not
   performed.

2. FINDINGS

   2.1  Membrane lap detailing at the north and east parapet terminations does not match the lap widths
        shown on the issued-for-construction roof detail. Laps appear to be approximately 50 mm narrower
        than specified at several locations.

   2.2  Roof drains at the low points of the north and east roof areas have a clear outlet area below the
        calculated design flow for the tributary roof area. Ponding was observed within 4 hours of rainfall
        at both locations.

   2.3  Staining consistent with water migration was observed on the ceiling of the third-floor tenant
        space at the north-east corner. No evidence of tenant alteration to the roof assembly was
        observed.

3. PROBABLE CAUSE

   In our opinion, the water ingress is most probably caused by (a) inadequate membrane lap detailing and
   (b) undersized roof drains. Both conditions are consistent with installation and design deficiencies.
   We cannot determine from the visual inspection alone whether the drain sizing originated in the design
   documents or in the installed outlets.

4. RECOMMENDATIONS

   4.1  Replace the roof drains at the north and east low points with drains sized to the design flow.
   4.2  Re-lap the membrane at the north and east parapet terminations to the issued-for-construction detail.
   4.3  Carry out a flood test of the affected roof areas before reinstating the tenant fit-out finishes.
