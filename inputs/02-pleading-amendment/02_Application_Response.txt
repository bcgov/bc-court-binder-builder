PART 2: ORDERS OPPOSED

The Respondents oppose the granting of the orders sought in paragraphs 2, 3 and 4 of Part 1 of the
Notice of Application, being the addition of Pro-Tech as a defendant, and the directions for service and
responsive pleadings that depend on that addition.

PART 3: ORDERS ON TERMS

In the alternative, if leave is granted to add Pro-Tech, the Respondents propose that:
(a) the Plaintiff pay the costs of the application thrown away, in any event of the cause;
(b) the time for Pro-Tech to file a Response to Civil Claim run from the date of service of the
    Amended Notice of Civil Claim, with an extension of forty-five (45) days from the date of this

(c) the Plaintiff particularize the allegations against Pro-Tech in a further amended pleading within
    fourteen (14) days, identifying each act of negligence and the specific roof components alleged to
    be defective.

PART 4: FACTUAL BASIS

1. The Plaintiff's claim was first pleaded on March 4, 2026, and the Defendant's Response to Civil Claim
   was filed on April 6, 2026. The Plaintiff has known of the water ingress since at least July 2025 and
   gave written notice of it to the Defendant on July 9, 2025. The Plaintiff therefore knew, or ought to
   have known, the material facts of its claim against the roofing contractor no later than that date.

2. The Plaintiff's proposed claim against Pro-Tech is based on an engineering inspection dated
   September 22, 2026, that the Plaintiff obtained more than fourteen (14) months after the water ingress
   was first documented. The inspection does not identify any new defect; it re-states a condition the
   Plaintiff had documented since 2025.

3. Pro-Tech did not design the building envelope and did not select the roof drainage layout. Those
   functions were performed by the architect and mechanical engineer engaged under the Prime Contract.
   The Proposed Amended Claim does not plead any particular act or omission by Pro-Tech that is distinct
   from the Defendant's subcontract scope.

4. Adding Pro-Tech at this stage would require Pro-Tech to investigate, retain its own engineering
   consultant, and respond to a claim that was not part of the original proceeding. Pro-Tech has no
   prior opportunity to preserve its own site records from the period of installation, which closed
   more than eighteen (18) months ago.

5. Under Supreme Court Civil Rule 6-2 the new party's presence must be necessary for the court to
   adjudicate the questions effectively and completely. The existing claim against the Defendant, as
   subcontractor-supervisor, can be determined without Pro-Tech being a party. The Plaintiff can pursue
   Pro-Tech in a separate action if it wishes, and the Respondents do not accept that a separate action
   would be a duplication.

PART 5: LEGAL BASIS

1. Supreme Court Civil Rule 6-1(1): leave to amend is not granted where the amendment is not properly
   particularized, discloses no reasonable claim, or would cause prejudice that cannot be compensated for
   by costs or by an adjournment.

2. Limitation Act, S.B.C. 2012, c. 13: the Plaintiff's knowledge of the material facts is the relevant
   date for the basic limitation period under section 6, and the Plaintiff's claim against Pro-Tech is
   out of time if the Plaintiff knew of the defect in 2025, and no later discoverability date is
   available on the evidence. The Respondents rely on the two-year basic period under section 6 and
   the discoverability provisions of section 8, and say the Plaintiff's own correspondence fixes the
   knowledge date.

3. Supreme Court Civil Rule 6-2 (adding parties): the joinder must be necessary, not merely convenient.

4. Practice Direction 73 (Virtual Chambers).

5. Chaston Construction Corp. v. Henderson Land, 2002 BCCA 357 (cited by the Plaintiff). The Respondents
   say the principle of liberal amendment is not a license to add a party whose limitation defence is
   apparent on the face of the record, and the authority tab in the Application Record is unverified.
