Part 1: ORDERS SOUGHT

1. Pursuant to Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(11), the defendant, North Shore 
   Logistics Inc., within fourteen (14) days of the date of this Order, prepare, 
   verify by affidavit, and serve upon the plaintiff an amended/supplementary 
   List of Documents listing all documents that are or have been in its possession 
   or control relating to the actual monthly inventory volume calculations and third-party 
   pallet throughput records for the period between January 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026.

2. Pursuant to Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1(18), the defendant produce for the 
   inspection of the plaintiff unredacted copies of the Internal Quarterly Operations 
   and Logistics Audit Reports prepared for the board of directors of North Shore 
   Logistics Inc. for Q1 2025 through Q2 2026 inclusive.

3. The costs of this application be awarded to the plaintiff in any event of the cause.


Part 2: FACTUAL BASIS

1. On October 14, 2024, the plaintiff and defendant executed a written Master Commercial 
   Warehousing & Logistics Agreement (the "Agreement"), under which the defendant agreed 
   to provide dedicated temperature-controlled storage and priority freight handling at 
   its Richmond terminal.

2. Clause 14.2 of the Agreement stipulates that warehousing fees payable by the plaintiff 
   are tied directly to verifiable pallet throughput thresholds, verified through monthly 
   third-party freight audits.

3. In this action, the plaintiff claims damages of $438,200.00 for systematic fee 
   overcharges and breach of the minimum available square footage covenant during the 
   18-month operating window between January 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026.

4. On August 18, 2026, the defendant delivered its initial List of Documents under Rule 7-1(1). 
   The list omitted all internal operations throughput logs and redacted the entirety 
   of its Quarterly Audit Reports on the ground of alleged "commercial sensitivity."

5. The defendant has refused, upon formal written demand delivered September 22, 2026, 
   to provide unredacted copies or supplement its List of Documents, despite these records 
   being directly material to the threshold inventory metrics in dispute.


Part 3: LEGAL BASIS

1. Supreme Court Civil Rules, B.C. Reg. 168/2009, Rules 7-1(1), 7-1(11), 7-1(18), 
   7-1(20), and 8-1.
2. The documents sought are directly material to proving whether the defendant possessed 
   the capacity it billed to the plaintiff and whether pallet calculations were inflated.
3. A claim of "commercial sensitivity" or competitive discomfort is not a recognized 
   ground of legal privilege under BC law: Kalin v. Campbell, 2014 BCCA 441 at paras 24-28.
4. The relief sought is proportionate within the meaning of Rule 1-3, as the production 
   requires no burdensome creation of new material, but merely unredacting and listing 
   discrete, existing operational records.


Part 4: MATERIAL TO BE RELIED ON

1. Affidavit #1 of Eleanor Vance, made October 14, 2026.
2. Pleadings filed herein.
3. Kalin v. Campbell, 2014 BCCA 441.
