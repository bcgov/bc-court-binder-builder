HEADNOTE / SUMMARY:
Practice — Civil procedure — Pre-trial document production — Rule 7-1 — 
Commercial sensitivity — Redactions — Proportionality.

The plaintiff appealed an interlocutory order denying unredacted production 
of quarterly operational reports and corporate management metrics. The chambers 
judge held that the documents contained proprietary competitive data and that 
disclosure was not proportionate under Rule 1-3.

Held: Appeal allowed. Commercial embarrassment or sensitivity does not constitute 
a recognised ground of privilege under the Supreme Court Civil Rules. Where 
documents are directly relevant to proving or disproving an allegation raised in 
the pleadings, they must be produced in unredacted form unless protected by legal 
professional or litigation privilege. If proprietary interests require protection, 
the appropriate mechanism is a confidentiality undertaking or protective order, 
not unilateral concealment.

REASONS FOR JUDGMENT OF THE HONOURABLE MR. JUSTICE WILLCOCK:

[24] The starting point under Supreme Court Civil Rule 7-1 is the obligation of 
a party to list and produce every document in its possession or control that could 
be used at trial to prove or disprove a material fact. The scope of production 
remains anchored in the issues defined by the pleadings.

[25] In the present case, the respondent justified extensive redactions to its 
management records on the basis that the redacted sections contained proprietary 
cost allocations and internal revenue margins that it wished to shield from a commercial 
competitor.

[26] With respect, commercial sensitivity is not privilege. While the court will 
be vigilant to prevent the discovery process from degenerating into abusive fishing 
expeditions designed to extract trade secrets, a party cannot unilaterally redact 
relevant portions of documents simply because disclosure would cause commercial 
discomfort or disclose financial calculations.

[27] As this Court emphasized in Biehl v. Strang, 2011 BCCA 215, proportionality 
under Rule 1-3 informs how discovery is conducted, but it does not extinguish the 
right of a litigant to inspect the core evidentiary foundation upon which the 
adverse party's billing or accounting formulas depend.

[28] If the records contain genuine trade secrets alongside material facts, the 
proper procedure is to seek a tailored confidentiality agreement or a protective 
order under Rule 7-1(18), restricting disclosure to counsel and designated experts. 
It was an error in principle to deny production altogether.

[29] The appeal is allowed with costs. The respondent is ordered to deliver 
unredacted copies of the disputed quarterly management reports within 14 days.
